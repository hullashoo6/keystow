KeyStow Year in Review: What Shipped in 2026

November 24, 2026 · KeyStow · All articles

Seven releases in the first week

KeyStow shipped its first release, v1.0.0, on November 17, 2026: push-to-talk voice typing for Windows and Mac. What followed was a dense first week of real-user fixes, and the changelog tells the story better than any summary could. Here it is in one place.

The releases

  • v1.0.0 (Nov 17). First release: hold a key, speak, release, and text lands where your cursor is.
  • v1.0.1 (Nov 18). Hold-to-speak became the default for new installs; existing custom keys kept their behavior.
  • v1.0.2 (Nov 19). A recovery update: paste-key cleanup, conditional clipboard restoration, local diagnostic logging, and safer handling of startup and sign-in registration.
  • v1.0.3 (Nov 20). Fixed duplicated dictation from multiple running copies: one KeyStow process per user, and closing the window now truly quits.
  • v1.0.4 (Nov 22). The activation key stopped waiting on microphone and disk work, stray sub-0.3-second taps are ignored, recordings cap at 120 seconds, clipboard restore slowed to fit slower apps, and start at login became a choice rather than a default.
  • v1.0.5 (Nov 22). Start at sign-in switched to on by default, as a ticked installer option on Windows that later updates remember.
  • v1.0.6 (Nov 22). The big structural fix: KeyStow now installs as ordinary files instead of unpacking about 500 MB into temp at every start. Started at sign-in it opens minimised. On Mac it gained the microphone permission entry macOS requires, Right Option as the default key, permission guidance in the window, and a clear message when exact silence reaches the microphone.

The honest footnote

The changelog carries its own warning, and it belongs in any honest recap: the Mac installers of v1.0.0 through v1.0.5 could not get microphone access. v1.0.6 is the first release whose Mac app can properly ask for it. Every release's notes also say plainly what was not yet tested on real hardware, and that practice continues.

What did not change

Through all seven releases the core promise stayed put: offline dictation on Windows and Apple Silicon Mac, your voice processed privately on your own computer and never sent to the cloud, no audio recording or transcript history kept, $5 once with no subscription and no account, and source code open under the MIT license.
