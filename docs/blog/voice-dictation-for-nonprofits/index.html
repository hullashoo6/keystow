Voice Dictation for Nonprofits: Grant Applications and Donor Updates

November 24, 2026 · KeyStow · All articles

Mission work does not pause for the writing

A nonprofit team of three does the work of ten, and the writing never fits: the grant narrative due Friday, the donor update that should have gone out last month, the volunteer instructions, the board summary. The stories that power fundraising are told all day on calls and site visits, then lost because nobody has time to type them.

Dictation captures the story where it happens. Speak the client anecdote (anonymized), the program win, the need, while it is fresh, and the grant draft starts from real material instead of a blank page.

What nonprofit teams dictate

  • Grant narratives. Answer each prompt out loud as if a program officer asked you directly, then edit for length and requirements.
  • Donor updates and appeals. Specific, warm, recent. "Here is what your support did this month" writes itself when you say it first.
  • Volunteer and event communications. Instructions, reminders, thank-yous.
  • Board and funder reports. The narrative around the numbers.

Respect the dollar and the data

Small nonprofits feel every subscription, and beneficiary stories are sensitive. KeyStow costs $5 once, with no subscription and no account. It processes your voice on your own computer and keeps no audio recording or transcript history; anonymize client details in anything you write, and follow your organization's privacy commitments.

How KeyStow fits

KeyStow is an offline voice dictation app for Windows and Mac. Hold Right Ctrl on Windows (Right Option on a Mac), speak, release, and the text is pasted where your cursor is, in the grant portal, your email or your docs. Your voice is processed privately on your own computer and never sent to the cloud, and dictation works without internet too. The source code is open under the MIT license.
