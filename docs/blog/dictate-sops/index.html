Dictating SOPs and Process Documentation Your Team Will Actually Read

November 24, 2026 · KeyStow · All articles

The documentation gap is a motivation gap

Every team has processes that live in one person's head. Everyone agrees they should be documented. Nobody documents them, because writing a procedure from memory, after the fact, is tedious in a way that doing the procedure never is.

The workaround: narrate while you work. Next time you run the process, dictate each step as you do it. "Open the billing panel, filter by failed payments, export the CSV." Fifteen minutes later the SOP exists, in plain language, in order, missing nothing.

How to dictate a usable SOP

  • Narrate in second person. "You open the panel, you click export." The reader is the next person to do this, possibly future you.
  • Say the why at decision points. "If the amount is over five hundred, loop in finance, because two people must approve refunds above that." Steps without reasons break the first time reality deviates.
  • Name screens and buttons exactly. Read the actual labels aloud. "The blue button" ages badly; "the Export button, top right" does not.
  • Add the failure modes after. Once the happy path is dictated, spend one minute on what goes wrong and who to ask.

Keeping SOPs alive

A dictated SOP is cheap to update, which matters more than being perfect on day one. When the process changes, the owner re-dictates the changed section in two minutes instead of letting the document rot.
