Voice Dictation for Novelists: Writing Fiction at Speaking Speed

November 24, 2026 · KeyStow · All articles

Dialogue wants to be spoken

Fiction has a secret: dialogue written by speaking it sounds like people talking, and dialogue typed word by word often sounds like typing. That is why authors have dictated for a century, to secretaries, to tape recorders, and now to software. The draft that comes out of your mouth already has rhythm; the edit adds polish.

Dictation also changes pace. A scene you would type in an afternoon can exist as a rough draft in an hour, rough being the operative word, but a rough scene you can revise beats a perfect scene you never start.

A workflow that works for fiction

  • Walk and talk the scene. Many authors draft pacing around, literally. Movement loosens dialogue.
  • Speak the action beats too. "She crosses the room, picks up the letter, doesn't open it." Stage directions come naturally in speech.
  • Mark, don't fix. When a sentence comes out wrong, say "new paragraph" and keep going. Editing mid-dictation kills the flow that makes dictation worth it.
  • Edit typing, not talking. The keyboard's turn comes after the draft exists.

Privacy for unfinished work

A novel in progress is unpublished intellectual property, and many authors are protective of it for good reason. Cloud dictation sends every spoken line to a third party. KeyStow runs the speech model on your own computer: your voice is processed privately on your own computer and never sent to the cloud, dictation works without internet too, and KeyStow does not keep an audio recording or transcript history.
