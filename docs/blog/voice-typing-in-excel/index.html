Voice Typing in Excel: Entering Text and Numbers by Voice

November 24, 2026 · KeyStow · All articles

Spreadsheets have more prose than you think

Excel work is not all formulas. Column labels, category names, comment threads, assumption notes, the "readme" tab nobody writes, status updates in a tracker: a surprising amount of spreadsheet content is plain text typed one cell at a time. That is the part dictation speeds up.

Where voice works well in Excel

  • Labels and categories. Setting up a new tracker or budget: speak the row and column names instead of pecking them.
  • Comments and notes. Reviewing someone's model? Dictate the comment while looking at the cell in question.
  • Assumption documentation. "Revenue grows at 5% because the contract renews in March": the note that saves the next person an hour, dictated in ten seconds.
  • Text-heavy trackers. Content calendars, CRM exports you annotate, bug lists, inventory descriptions.

Where to keep the keyboard

Formulas, precise number entry and anything where a misheard digit is expensive. "Five point two" and "5.2" are a dictation ambiguity you do not want in a financial model. Dictate the prose, type the figures, and always read number cells with your eyes.

How KeyStow fits

KeyStow is an offline voice dictation app for Windows and Apple Silicon Mac. Hold Right Ctrl on Windows (Right Option on a Mac), speak, release, and the text is typed into the active cell or comment box. Your voice is processed privately on your own computer and never sent to the cloud, and dictation works without internet too, so unreleased financials stay on your machine. KeyStow does not keep an audio recording or transcript history. It costs $5 once, with no subscription and no account, and the source code is open under the MIT license.
