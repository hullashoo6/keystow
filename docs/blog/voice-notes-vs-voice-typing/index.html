Voice Notes vs Voice Typing: When to Record and When to Dictate

November 24, 2026 · KeyStow · All articles

Two different tools people confuse

"Voice notes" and "voice typing" both use your microphone, but they solve opposite problems:

  • A voice note is a recording. You capture audio now and deal with it later. It preserves everything, including the parts you did not mean.
  • Voice typing (dictation) turns speech into text in the moment. There is no recording to manage; there is just text where your cursor is.

Choosing wrong is how people end up with 47 unlistened voice memos and nothing searchable.

Record when the audio itself matters

  • Interviews and meetings (with consent, and following recording laws): you need the exact words and the option to replay tone.
  • Ideas while walking or driving: capture now, transcribe later. A raw thought at walking pace is better as audio than as a distracted dictation attempt.
  • Anything you might need to prove: recordings are evidence; transcripts are summaries.

Dictate when the text is the goal

  • Drafts: emails, essays, docs, posts. If the end product is text, start in text.
  • Messages: chat replies, comments, form fields. Recording a voice note for these just moves typing to the listener.
  • Notes you will search later: text is searchable on day one. Audio is searchable never, unless someone transcribes it.

The trap: voice notes as procrastination

Voice notes feel productive because speaking is easy. But a recording defers every decision: what mattered, how to phrase it, where it goes. Dictation forces those decisions in real time, which is exactly why the output is more useful. If your voice-memo app is a graveyard, switch the capture to dictation into a notes app and let the text be the record.
