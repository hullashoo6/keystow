Voice Dictation for Executives: Memos, Decisions and the Return of the Dictaphone Habit

November 24, 2026 · KeyStow · All articles

The oldest executive productivity tool

Senior people used to dictate everything: letters, memos, instructions. It was not a quirk; it was the rational division of a scarce resource. An executive's value is judgment expressed in words, and typing was never a good use of those words. Somewhere along the way the keyboard ate the habit and executives started personally typing their own long emails at 11pm.

Modern dictation restores the old division of labor without the secretary: speak, and the words are typed.

Where dictation fits an executive's day

  • Decision memos: talking through a decision out loud ("here is what we know, here is what I am deciding, here is why") is the fastest way to find out whether the reasoning holds. The recording-quality draft is a byproduct.
  • The long, important email: company-wide notes, board updates, the careful message after hard news. These deserve a spoken first pass; you can hear immediately whether it sounds like leadership or like a form letter.
  • Meeting follow-ups: commitments and direction, dictated in the two minutes after the meeting, actually get sent.
  • Interview and hiring feedback: impressions captured fresh instead of reconstructed at panel time.

The confidentiality calculus at the top

Executive speech is the most sensitive audio in the company: strategy, personnel matters, M&A, legal exposure. Dictating that into a cloud service is a decision no CISO would approve casually. The small experiment

Tomorrow morning, dictate your first email instead of typing it. That is the whole experiment. The habit your grandfather's office had figured out tends to re-establish itself quickly.
