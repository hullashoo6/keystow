Voice Typing in Microsoft Word: Built-in Dictate vs an Offline Alternative

November 24, 2026 · KeyStow · All articles

Word's built-in Dictate

Microsoft Word has a Dictate button in the ribbon (and voice typing across Windows via Win+H). It is genuinely convenient for many people. Two things to know before you build a workflow on it:

1. It needs the internet. Microsoft's support page is clear that Windows voice typing relies on online speech services. No connection, no dictation.

2. It lives where Microsoft puts it. Dictate is a Word and Microsoft 365 feature; the Windows-wide Win+H variant covers other apps, but both share the same online dependency and Microsoft's privacy terms for speech.

If you write in Word at a desk with solid wifi, the built-in button may be all you need. This post is for everyone else.

When you want something different

  • You draft on the move. Planes, trains, client sites with locked-down guest wifi: an online service fails exactly when you have time to write.
  • You write in many apps. A system-wide tool types into Word, your browser, your email, your notes: one habit everywhere.
  • Your words are sensitive. Contracts, HR notes, client matters. Sending raw voice to a cloud service is a policy question at many companies; keeping it local avoids the question.

The offline alternative: KeyStow

KeyStow is a $5 once, lifetime dictation app for Windows and Apple Silicon Mac that runs Whisper speech models entirely on your machine. Hold the shortcut key, talk, release: text lands wherever your cursor is, including Word.

  • Offline: works with the internet off, every time.
  • Private: your voice is processed privately on your own computer and never sent to the cloud. Dictation works without internet too. KeyStow does not keep an audio recording or transcript history.
  • Everywhere: not tied to Word, not tied to Windows. Same key in every app on both operating systems.
  • Free alternatives: Handy (free, open source, offline push-to-talk for Windows, macOS and Linux) and Superwhisper (free local tier, Pro from $8.49 per month) also process locally.

A hybrid habit that works

Plenty of writers use both: Word's Dictate at the desk when it is convenient, KeyStow as the always-there default everywhere else, especially offline. Habits beat features: the tool you reach for without thinking is the one that actually saves you time.
