Brainstorming Out Loud: Solo Ideation by Voice

November 24, 2026 · KeyStow · All articles

Your inner critic types slower than you think

Solo brainstorming has a built-in flaw: the same hands that write the idea also judge it. By the time "what if we..." reaches the keyboard, half the ideas have been vetoed as silly, and silly is where the good ones hide. Typing is slow enough to let the editor win.

Talking has no such gap. Ideas arrive at speaking speed, half-formed and honest, and dictation catches all of them. The judgment can come later, with coffee, reading a page of raw material instead of three polished lines.

How to run a twenty-minute voice brainstorm

  • Set one question. Not "think about the launch" but "what are ten ways to announce the launch?"
  • Dictate without stopping. Number the ideas out loud. Do not explain, do not apologize, do not backspace. Quantity now, quality later.
  • Chase the weird ones. When an idea surprises you, stay on it for three more sentences. Surprises are the point.
  • Stop at twenty minutes. Then read the transcript and mark the two ideas worth a second look.

What this is good for

Naming things, headlines, product angles, plot turns, talk topics, gift ideas, the email you are dreading: anything where the first ten answers are obvious and the eleventh is interesting.
