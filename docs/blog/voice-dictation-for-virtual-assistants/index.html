Voice Dictation for Virtual Assistants: Client Work at Speaking Speed

November 24, 2026 · KeyStow · All articles

The VA bottleneck is typing, not thinking

A virtual assistant's value is judgment: knowing what the client would say, which email matters, how this vendor should be handled. The typing is overhead. When you manage three clients' inboxes, calendars and documents, the minutes spent typing are the minutes that cap how many clients you can serve well.

Dictation removes the overhead. Replies, summaries and drafts go out at speaking speed, in the client's voice, not a template's.

What virtual assistants dictate

  • Inbox triage replies. Read, decide, speak the reply, quick check, send. The human tone survives because you literally said it.
  • Research summaries. "Here are the three options and my recommendation," dictated in two minutes after an hour of reading.
  • Meeting notes and recaps. Client calls become action lists while you still remember the nuance.
  • Document drafts. SOPs, itineraries, onboarding guides: the structured writing clients dread and VAs are paid to produce.

Working in client systems

VAs type into whatever the client uses: Gmail, Outlook, Notion, Asana, a practice-management portal. A dictation tool that only works in one app is useless; the text has to land where the cursor is. It also has to respect client confidentiality: voice processed on your own computer, never sent to a cloud service, with no recordings or transcripts kept. Client agreements still govern the tools you use, so check them.

How KeyStow fits

KeyStow is an offline voice dictation app for Windows and Apple Silicon Mac. Hold Right Ctrl on Windows (Right Option on a Mac), speak, release, and the text is pasted into whichever app is focused. Your voice is processed privately on your own computer and never sent to the cloud, and dictation works without internet too. KeyStow does not keep an audio recording or transcript history.
