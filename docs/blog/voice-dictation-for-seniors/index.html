Voice Dictation for Older Adults: A Gentle Way to Write Without Typing

November 24, 2026 · KeyStow · All articles

The keyboard should not be a gatekeeper

Email to the grandchildren, the letter to the insurance company, the family history you keep meaning to write down. None of it is hard to say. For many people, the typing is the hard part, whether because of arthritis, eyesight, or simply never having learned to type fast. That is a bad reason for thoughts to go unwritten.

Voice dictation removes the keyboard from the equation: hold one key, talk, let go. The words appear on the screen, in the email or document you already have open.

Getting started, gently

  • Start with one real email. Not practice: a short note to someone you like. Purpose makes the habit stick.
  • Speak at conversation pace. There is no need to talk slowly or loudly. Normal speech works best.
  • Expect small mistakes. Names and unusual words sometimes come out wrong. Read the text once before sending, exactly as you would a typed letter.
  • Ask family for the ten-minute setup. Installing the app is a one-time favor; using it afterwards is one key.

What to write by voice first

Emails and letters are the natural start. After that: recipes for the family book, memories and stories for the grandchildren, club newsletters, complaint letters that deserve your real voice. Many people find their dictated letters sound more like them than their typed ones ever did.

How KeyStow fits

KeyStow is an offline voice dictation app for Windows and Apple Silicon Mac computers. Hold Right Ctrl on Windows (Right Option on a Mac), speak, release, and the text is pasted where your cursor is. Your voice is processed privately on your own computer and never sent to the cloud, and dictation works without internet too. KeyStow does not keep an audio recording or transcript history.
