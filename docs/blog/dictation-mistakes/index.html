Common Dictation Mistakes New Users Make (and the Fixes)

November 24, 2026 · KeyStow · All articles

Mistakes are part of the learning curve

Nearly everyone makes the same five mistakes in their first week of dictation. They are all easy to fix once you can name them.

The five usual suspects

  • Mumbling. Dictation rewards the clear, slightly deliberate speech you would use with a colleague on a bad phone line. You do not need to shout; you need to finish your words.
  • Releasing the key on the last syllable. With hold-to-talk, letting go too early clips your final word. Hold a beat past the end of the sentence, then release.
  • Editing while speaking. Stopping mid-sentence to fix a word breaks your spoken rhythm and produces worse text than speaking through and cleaning up after. Draft by voice, edit by keyboard.
  • Expecting a finished document. Dictation produces a fast first draft, not a polished one. Judging it by final-draft standards is how people quit a tool that was working.
  • Dictating into noise and blaming the model. A spinning fan, a TV in the next room or a headset rubbing against clothing all cost accuracy. Quieter input beats a bigger model.

The habits that replace them

  • Speak in complete thoughts. Sentences, not fragments, give the model context to work with.
  • Do one quiet test paragraph in each new app or room before trusting it with real work.
  • Keep the keyboard close. The fastest dictation users are bilingual: voice for volume, keys for surgery.
