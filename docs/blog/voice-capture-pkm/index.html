Capture Everything by Voice: Dictation and Personal Knowledge Management

November 24, 2026 · KeyStow · All articles

The core problem of note-taking is capture speed

Every note-taking system, from a paper notebook to an elaborate PKM setup, lives or dies on one thing: whether thoughts actually get captured at the moment they occur. Ideas arrive while walking, cooking, or in the shower, and the friction of writing them down kills most of them. Typing is the bottleneck between having a thought and keeping it.

Dictation is the fastest capture method that produces searchable text. Hold a key, say the thought, release: it is in your notes app, in your own words, findable forever.

Capture habits that work by voice

  • The idea dump: when a thought arrives, dictate two sentences. Not polished, just captured. The two-sentence version is infinitely more useful than the idea you trusted to memory.
  • Reading notes: after each chapter or article, dictate what it said and why you care. Your own words, immediately, is the whole point of the exercise.
  • Journaling: a spoken day-entry is the lowest-friction journaling method there is. Three sentences spoken nightly beats a beautiful journal started four times.
  • Weekly review: dictate your reflection on the week into your notes; the spoken review surfaces patterns typing would have filtered out.

Why your PKM audio should stay local

A personal knowledge system is precisely that: personal. It holds your unfiltered thinking, half-formed ideas, private reflections, things about other people. A cloud dictation tool processes all of it on third-party servers. KeyStow ($5 once, lifetime, Windows and Mac) runs its Whisper model offline on your machine: your voice is processed privately on your own computer and never sent to the cloud, works without internet, and keeps no audio recording or transcript history. Your raw thinking stays exactly where the notes do.

One boundary

Dictation captures prose. It will not transcribe a recorded lecture or interview for you (that is transcription, a different job), and it does not organize your notes; that part is still yours. But the thoughts will exist to be organized, which is the part most systems never get to.
