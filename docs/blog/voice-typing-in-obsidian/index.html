Voice Typing in Obsidian: Local Notes Meet Local Dictation

November 24, 2026 · KeyStow · All articles

The local-first pairing

Obsidian people chose their notes app on principle: plain Markdown files, on their own disk, no dependency on someone's cloud. Dictation deserves the same standard. A cloud dictation service would re-route every spoken note through a third party on its way into your otherwise-local vault.

The natural pairing is offline dictation: the speech model runs on your computer, the text lands in the note, and nothing leaves the machine at any step.

A capture workflow for your vault

  • Fleeting notes by voice. Idea, quote, todo: open today's daily note, dictate two lines, move on. Capture speed is the whole game.
  • Book and article notes. After each reading session, talk through what stuck and why. Two minutes of voice beats a highlight graveyard.
  • Weekly review drafts. Speak the week's wins and loose ends into the review note, then structure them with your usual templates.
  • Draft in longform when it flows. Obsidian handles big documents fine, and a dictated first draft is the fastest way to fill one.

Practical notes

Obsidian accepts pasted text anywhere, which is exactly how offline dictation delivers it: hold a key, speak, release, and the text is pasted at the cursor. Links, tags and headings still get added with the keyboard; that split of labor is the workflow.

How KeyStow fits

KeyStow is an offline voice dictation app for Windows and Mac. Hold Right Ctrl on Windows (Right Option on a Mac), speak, release, and the text is pasted into the open note. Your voice is processed privately on your own computer and never sent to the cloud, and dictation works without internet too. KeyStow does not keep an audio recording or transcript history. It costs $5 once, with no subscription and no account, and the source code is open under the MIT license, the same auditability standard as your vault.
