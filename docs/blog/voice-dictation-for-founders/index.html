Voice Dictation for Founders: Investor Updates and Decision Logs by Voice

November 24, 2026 · KeyStow · All articles

The update that goes out late every month

Every founder knows the loop: the month ends, the investor update is due, and it goes out on the 9th because writing it is a chore. The numbers are in a dashboard; the missing part is the narrative: what changed, what you learned, what you need. That narrative is a conversation you have had a dozen times. Saying it once more, into text, takes minutes.

The same applies to the internal writing founders skip: the decision log entry, the "why we are doing this" memo, the retro.

What founders dictate

  • Monthly investor updates. Highlights, lowlights, asks, metrics context. Dictate the narrative around the numbers and edit once.
  • Decision logs. What you decided, why, and what would change your mind. Sixty seconds of voice per decision, invaluable six months later.
  • Strategy memos. Talking through a bet out loud is the fastest way to find the holes in it; the transcript is the first draft of the memo.
  • Board prep. The story of the quarter in your own voice before it becomes slides.

Why the habit sticks better than a template

Templates fail because staring at a blank template is still writing. Dictation lowers the activation energy to "hold a key and explain it like you would to a friend." A founder who can explain the business over coffee can produce the update over coffee.

How KeyStow fits

KeyStow is an offline voice dictation app for Windows and Mac. Hold Right Ctrl on Windows (Right Option on a Mac), speak, release, and the text is pasted where your cursor is. Your voice is processed privately on your own computer and never sent to the cloud, and dictation works without internet too, which matters when the draft mentions unreleased numbers. KeyStow does not keep an audio recording or transcript history. It costs $5 once, with no subscription and no account, and the source code is open under the MIT license.
