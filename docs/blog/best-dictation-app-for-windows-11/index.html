Best Dictation App for Windows 11: Offline Options

November 24, 2026 · KeyStow · All articles

What "best" means on Windows 11

Windows 11 already ships with voice typing, and the app store is full of dictation tools that look identical in screenshots. The differences that matter show up elsewhere: where your audio is processed, what the tool costs after the first month, and whether it works when your connection does not. This roundup compares the options on those axes, with links to each maker's own pages so you can verify every claim.

Windows voice typing (built in, free)

Press Win+H in any text field and Windows 11 transcribes your speech. It costs nothing and requires no install. Two things to know: Microsoft's support page says voice typing relies on an online speech service, so it needs an internet connection, and your audio is processed by Microsoft's cloud rather than on your PC. For occasional short snippets on a good connection, it is genuinely convenient. For long sessions, private material, or unreliable internet, it is the wrong tool.

KeyStow ($5 once, lifetime)

KeyStow is a small Windows app built around local Whisper speech models. Hold a key (Right Ctrl on Windows, Right Option on a Mac by default; changeable), talk, release, and the text appears in whatever app you are using. Everything runs on your PC: your voice is processed privately on your own computer and never sent to the cloud, and dictation works without internet too. KeyStow does not keep an audio recording or transcript history.

It costs $5 once for a lifetime license, with no account and no subscription, and it is MIT open source. It also runs on Mac. For Windows 11 users who want dictation that behaves like a local utility rather than a service, this is the straightforward pick.

Handy (free, open source)

Handy is a free, MIT-licensed push-to-talk dictation app for Windows, macOS and Linux that runs speech models locally. Press the shortcut, speak, get text; options like multiple speech models, a custom dictionary and transcript history are there for those who want them. If your budget is exactly zero and you want offline dictation on Windows 11, Handy is the first thing to try. Its GitHub repository is the source of truth for features and setup.

Wispr Flow (free tier, Pro from $12 per user per month)

Wispr Flow is the polished, cloud-based option. It supports Windows, Mac, iPhone and Android, cleans up filler words and formats as you speak. The trade-offs are structural: its privacy page states that transcription always happens in the cloud, so your voice leaves your PC and a connection is required, and the full product is a subscription ($15 per user per month billed monthly, $12 per user per month billed annually, per its pricing page). There is a free tier to test it with.

Superwhisper (free local tier, Pro from $8.49 per month)

Superwhisper processes audio locally and offers a free tier, with Pro at $8.49 per month, $84.99 per year, or a $249.99 lifetime plan per its billing docs. It runs on Windows as well as Mac and iPhone. A solid option if you want local processing with a wider set of model and style choices, and do not mind paying for the advanced features.

Dragon Professional v16 ($699)

Dragon was the default answer to this question for two decades, and it still exists: Nuance sells Dragon Professional v16, optimized for Windows 11, at $699 from its own store and retailers. It is accurate and deeply customizable, but at that price it makes sense mainly for professionals whose work already depends on it. For everyone else, the tools above cover the same ground for far less money.

How to choose

  • Want free and offline: Handy.
  • Want offline, polished, and cheap forever: KeyStow ($5 once).
  • Want cloud features, styles and mobile apps, and accept subscriptions: Wispr Flow or Superwhisper.
  • Want zero installs for occasional use: Win+H.
