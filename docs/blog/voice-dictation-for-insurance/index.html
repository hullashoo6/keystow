Voice Dictation for Insurance and Claims Professionals: Accurate Notes Without the Keyboard

November 24, 2026 · KeyStow · All articles

The claim file is the product

Adjusters and claims professionals know: if it is not in the file, it did not happen. Scene notes, statements, reserve rationales, follow-up logs, denial and approval explanations. The writing is constant, it is judged on accuracy, and most of it is typed at the end of a long day.

Dictating notes right after an inspection or call keeps the record specific: times, positions, exact words, the detail that matters six months later.

What claims professionals dictate

  • Inspection and scene notes. Walk the site and describe what you see while seeing it.
  • Call summaries. What the claimant said, what you committed to, what happens next, captured in the minute after hanging up.
  • Rationale paragraphs. The reasoning behind a reserve or a decision, spoken as if explaining it to your supervisor, then tightened.
  • Follow-up letters and emails. Clear, dated, specific.

Accuracy habits that are non-negotiable

Claim notes get read by counsel, regulators and courts. Dictate the prose, then verify every date, amount, name and policy reference with your eyes before it enters the file. Dictation speeds up capture; your review keeps the record exact.

A note on compliance

KeyStow processes your voice on your own computer and keeps no audio recording or transcript history. That is a meaningful privacy property, but it does not by itself make any tool compliant with your employer's policies, your state's recording rules, or industry regulation. Those rules decide what you may use; check them first.

How KeyStow fits

KeyStow is an offline voice dictation app for Windows and Apple Silicon Mac. Hold Right Ctrl on Windows (Right Option on a Mac), speak, release, and the text is typed where your cursor is. Your voice is processed privately on your own computer and never sent to the cloud, and dictation works without internet too, which matters at loss sites.
