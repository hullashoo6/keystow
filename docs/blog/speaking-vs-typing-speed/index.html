Speaking vs Typing Speed: What the Numbers Mean for Your Day

November 24, 2026 · KeyStow · All articles

The gap everyone quotes

Ask around and you will hear that speaking is three or four times faster than typing. The typical ranges behind that claim: most people type somewhere around 40 words per minute, and conversational speech runs well above 100. These are typical ranges from typing tests and linguistics research, not benchmarks of you, and both numbers vary widely by person and task.

Even with every caveat, the gap is large. The interesting question is not whether it exists but what it does and does not buy you.

Where the speed is real

  • First drafts. This is the clean win. A page of rough draft that takes twenty minutes to type takes a few minutes to speak.
  • Notes and capture. Speed matters most when the alternative is not writing it down at all.
  • Volume work. Thirty similar emails, a dozen product descriptions, a week of reports: small per-item savings compound.

Where the speed is not real

  • Finished prose. Add the edit and the multiple shrinks. Dictation plus editing still beats typing for most people on drafts of any length, but the honest advantage is closer to "much faster" than "four times faster."
  • Precision work. Numbers, code, formatted documents: the keyboard wins, and forcing voice here is a hobby, not a hack.
  • Thinking time. If you did not know what to say, no input method saves you. Dictation accelerates articulation, not thought.

The honest bottom line

For drafting prose, speaking is dramatically faster than typing, and the gap survives the edit. That is worth real hours to anyone whose job is writing-shaped. The way to find out what it is worth to you specifically: dictate your next first draft and time it.
