Voice Dictation for Lawyers: Confidential Drafting and the Cloud Question

November 24, 2026 · KeyStow · All articles

Law was built on dictation

Lawyers dictated to secretaries, then to Dictaphones, for a century. The modern version is software that types as you talk, and the productivity case is the same as ever: lawyers think in spoken argument, and briefs, memos, demand letters and client emails all start as things the lawyer could simply say.

What changed is where the audio goes

The old dictaphone's tape sat in your office. Modern cloud dictation sends your voice to a vendor's servers for processing. For a profession where client confidentiality and privilege are foundational, that is not a detail. Dictating "re: the merger, our exposure is..." into a cloud service means a third party processed the substance of a matter.

Local dictation removes that transfer. KeyStow ($5 once, lifetime, Windows and Apple Silicon Mac) runs Whisper speech models entirely on the computer in front of you: your voice is processed privately on your own machine and never sent to the cloud, it works without internet, and no audio recording or transcript history is kept. Handy (free, open source) is another offline option.

Two limits, stated plainly:

1. Local processing is not compliance. It answers where the audio goes. It does not, by itself, satisfy your jurisdiction's rules on technology and client confidentiality, your firm's policies, or your ethical obligations. Those still require your own review.

2. The text you produce is still client work product. Where you store and send it matters as much as how you drafted it.

Where dictation fits a legal practice

  • First drafts of briefs and memos: argue it out loud, then edit. Spoken argument is the native format of legal reasoning.
  • Client emails: the careful, plain-language explanation is easier to get right when spoken first.
  • Post-hearing and post-meeting notes: capture impressions immediately, while they are admissible to your own memory.
  • Time-entry narratives: dictate them at the end of the day instead of reconstructing them at the end of the month.
