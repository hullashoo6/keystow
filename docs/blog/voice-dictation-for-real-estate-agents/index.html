Voice Dictation for Real Estate Agents: Listing Descriptions and Client Follow-Ups

November 24, 2026 · KeyStow · All articles

Agents write everywhere except a desk

Real estate writing happens in the gaps: after a showing, in the driveway, between appointments. Listing descriptions, showing notes, client follow-ups and offer summaries all pile up for the evening, when the details have gone cold. Typing on a phone is slow, and a laptop does not balance on a steering wheel.

Dictation flips the order. You speak the listing description while the property is still in front of you, and the text is waiting when you get back to the office.

What agents dictate

  • Listing descriptions. Walk the property and describe what you see in your selling voice. The spoken draft already sounds like you talk to buyers, which is usually a better listing tone than typed prose.
  • Showing notes. Thirty seconds after each showing: what the buyers loved, what they hesitated on, what to send them. Said out loud, it is captured; left for later, it is lost.
  • Follow-up messages. Personalized replies to clients while the conversation is fresh, pasted into your mail app or CRM notes field.
  • Offer and counter-offer summaries. The plain-English recap you send the client before the paperwork.

Why offline matters for field work

Site visits do not come with reliable Wi-Fi. New construction, basements and rural listings are exactly where connections drop. A dictation tool that depends on the cloud stops working at the moment field notes matter most, and a cloud tool also sends your clients' details off your computer.

How KeyStow fits

KeyStow is an offline voice dictation app for Windows and Apple Silicon Mac. Hold Right Ctrl on Windows (Right Option on a Mac), speak, release, and the text is pasted where your cursor is, in any app. Your voice is processed privately on your own computer and never sent to the cloud, and dictation works without internet too. KeyStow does not keep an audio recording or transcript history.

The speech model ships inside the installer, so there is no model download on first use. It costs $5 once, with no subscription and no account, and the source code is open under the MIT license.
