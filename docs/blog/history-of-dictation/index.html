A Short History of Dictation: From Secretaries to Speech Models

November 24, 2026 · KeyStow · All articles

When dictation meant a person

For most of history, dictation was a job description. Executives, doctors and lawyers spoke; a stenographer captured it in shorthand and typed it up. Speaking your writing was normal office life for a century, long before anyone called it a productivity hack.

The machines arrive

The Dictaphone (early 1900s) let you record speech onto wax cylinders for later transcription, splitting speaking from typing in time for the first time. Tape recorders and microcassette dictaphones kept the pattern alive for decades: doctors dictated case notes, lawyers dictated letters, and transcription pools typed them up.

Software takes a turn

By the 1990s and 2000s, computer speech recognition became a real product. Dragon NaturallySpeaking (now Dragon Professional) made "talk to your computer and it types" something an individual could buy, train, and use, no stenographer required. It worked, but it was expensive, Windows-only, and demanded patience and training.

Then the smartphone era put cloud dictation in everyone's pocket. Siri, Google Voice Typing, and built-in dictation on Windows (Win+H) and Mac made speaking to devices routine, but the speech traveled to company servers for processing.

The open-source turn

In 2022, OpenAI released Whisper: an open-source speech recognition model family, MIT-licensed, accurate enough for real work, and small enough to run on an ordinary laptop. That changed the economics and the privacy model at once. Good dictation no longer required a $699 license or a cloud server. Tools like Handy (free, open source) and KeyStow ($5 once, lifetime) built on Whisper to offer offline dictation where your voice never leaves your computer.

Where this goes

The pattern across a century: dictation keeps shedding intermediaries. Person to machine to cloud to the device already on your desk. The latest step, an offline model on your own laptop, is the first one where speaking your writing is simultaneously cheap, private, and entirely under your control. A hundred years of dictation history ends with you, a microphone, and nobody else listening.
