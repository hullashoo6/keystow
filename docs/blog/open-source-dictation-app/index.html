The Best Open-Source Dictation Apps Built on Whisper

November 24, 2026 · KeyStow · All articles

OpenAI released Whisper as an open-source speech recognition model in 2022, under the MIT license. It is accurate, it runs on ordinary computers, and it sparked a whole category of apps that transcribe your voice locally, with no cloud account and no per-month fee. If you like software you can inspect, modify and keep, these are the open-source dictation apps worth knowing in 2026.

KeyStow (MIT) - $5 for the packaged app

KeyStow is an offline voice dictation application for Windows and Mac, built for one job: hold a key, speak, and get text at your cursor. It runs Whisper models locally in several sizes, starts when you sign in, sits in the system tray, and keeps no audio recording or transcript history. The code is MIT-licensed on GitHub. The $5 price buys the ready-to-install Windows and Mac builds with lifetime updates; anyone comfortable building from source can read exactly what the app does first.

Handy (MIT) - free

Handy is a free, cross-platform speech-to-text app for Windows, macOS and Linux. Press a shortcut, speak, release, and your words land in the text field you are using. It is free and open source (MIT), with options like transcript history and a custom dictionary. A good pick if you want something completely free.

Buzz (MIT) - for transcribing files and recordings

Buzz takes a different angle: instead of live dictation at your cursor, it transcribes and translates audio offline on your computer. If your job is turning recordings into text rather than speaking into documents, Buzz is the open-source option to look at.

Why open source matters for dictation

  • You can verify the privacy claim. "Your voice stays on your computer" is a big promise. With open source, anyone can read the code and check what is actually sent, stored or logged. KeyStow and Handy both make this checkable.
  • No subscription lock-in. The model is free (MIT), and the apps above are free or a few dollars once. There is no meter running.
  • Longevity. If a company shuts down a cloud dictation service, it stops working. An app with a local model and public source keeps working, and the community can maintain it.

Which one should you try?

  • Want polished hold-to-talk dictation on Windows or Mac for the price of a coffee: KeyStow, $5 once.
  • Want completely free and open source: Handy.
  • Want to transcribe audio files offline: Buzz.
