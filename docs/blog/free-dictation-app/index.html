Free vs Paid Dictation Apps: What You Actually Get

November 24, 2026 · KeyStow · All articles

The real price of "free"

Every dictation app claims to be free at the door. The differences are in what happens after: some tools are free forever, some are free until you want the features you saw in the screenshots, and some are free only because your voice is the product's raw material. Here is what each tier actually buys, with links to the makers' own pages.

What $0 gets you

Windows voice typing (Win+H) and Apple Dictation are already on your computer. They cost nothing and handle short snippets well. Microsoft's support page notes that Windows voice typing uses an online speech service, so it needs internet and sends audio to the cloud. Apple's page notes that on-device processing depends on your Mac and settings. Convenience: high. Control: limited.

Handy is the standout of the genuinely free tier: an open-source (MIT), offline, push-to-talk app for Windows, macOS and Linux. Local models, no account, no upsell screen. If you want private dictation and your budget is zero, install Handy first.

Free tiers of paid apps (Wispr Flow, Superwhisper) are trials by another name. Superwhisper's free tier does run locally, which is unusual and welcome. Wispr Flow's free tier runs in the cloud like its paid plans. Both are designed to show you what the paid version feels like.

What $5 gets you

KeyStow costs $5 once, for a lifetime license. For that you get the thing most people actually want from dictation: hold a key, talk, and text appears in any app. The Whisper speech models run locally on your computer, so your voice is processed privately on your own computer and never sent to the cloud, and dictation works without internet too. KeyStow does not keep an audio recording or transcript history. It runs on Windows and Mac, starts at sign-in, and is MIT open source. There is no Pro tier because there is nothing held back.

What a subscription gets you

Wispr Flow Pro is $15 per user per month billed monthly, or $12 per user per month billed annually, per its pricing page. For that you get cloud transcription with automatic editing and formatting, on Mac, Windows, iPhone and Android. Its privacy page states that transcription always happens in the cloud, which is the trade you make for those features.

Superwhisper Pro is $8.49 per month, $84.99 per year, or $249.99 lifetime, per its billing docs. Processing stays local; the subscription buys a wider choice of models, custom modes and active development.

Over three years, a $12-per-month subscription adds up to $432. That is not automatically a bad deal; it is simply a different product: a service with ongoing development and sync, versus a utility you buy once.

What $699 gets you

Dragon Professional v16 is the legacy heavyweight: $699 from Nuance's store, optimized for Windows 11, with deep customization for specialist vocabularies. It earns its price in medical, legal and enterprise settings. For everyday writing, it is more product than most people need.

The honest summary

  • Free and offline exists and is good: Handy.
  • $5 once buys a polished, private, offline tool with nothing held back: KeyStow.
  • Subscriptions buy cloud features, styling and mobile apps, and charge you every month for them.
  • $699 buys professional-grade specialization most writers will never use.

Pick the row that matches how you actually work, not the row with the longest feature list.
