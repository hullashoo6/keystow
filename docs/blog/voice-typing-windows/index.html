How to Type With Your Voice in Any App on Windows

November 24, 2026 · KeyStow · All articles

Voice typing is not just for one app or one website. Set it up once, and you can speak text into Word, Gmail, Slack, Notepad, a browser form, anywhere your cursor can blink. Here are the two ways to do it on Windows, and when each makes sense.

Option 1: Windows' built-in voice typing

Windows 11 has voice typing built in:

  • Click into any text field.
  • Press the Windows logo key + H.
  • Speak. Your words appear at the cursor. Press Win+H again or click the microphone to stop.

Two things to know, from Microsoft's own support page: voice typing uses online speech recognition powered by Azure Speech services, and you need an internet connection. So your audio goes to Microsoft's cloud, and it stops working when your connection does.

Option 2: A dedicated offline app (KeyStow)

If you want voice typing that keeps audio on your PC and works without internet, a local app is the other route. With KeyStow:

  • Install KeyStow (no admin needed, no account).
  • Click into any text field, in any app.
  • Hold Right Ctrl, speak, release. The text is pasted at your cursor.

Because KeyStow pastes into whatever field has focus, it works the same in every app: documents, email, chat, code editors, browser forms. The activation key can be changed to Right Shift, Right Alt or another key, and the app starts automatically when you sign in, so it is always one key away. Transcription uses OpenAI's Whisper models running on your PC, so nothing is uploaded and it works on airplane Wi-Fi, which is to say, no Wi-Fi at all.

Which should you use?

For a quick note once a week, Win+H is already on your PC. For daily dictation, or anything you would rather not send to a server, KeyStow is $5 once and keeps it local.
