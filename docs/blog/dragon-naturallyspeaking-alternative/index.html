Dragon NaturallySpeaking Alternatives in 2026

November 24, 2026 · KeyStow · All articles

What happened to Dragon NaturallySpeaking

For two decades, "Dragon" was synonymous with dictation. The lineup has changed. Nuance (now part of Microsoft) retired the NaturallySpeaking name and the consumer and Mac versions; what remains for Windows is Dragon Professional v16, sold at $699 from Nuance's own store and positioned for business. If you come back to dictation after a few years away and go looking for the old Dragon box, this is why you cannot find it.

The good news: speech recognition moved on. Modern models, especially OpenAI's open-source Whisper family, made accurate dictation cheap to build and cheap to sell. The tools below cover what Dragon did for everyday writers, most of them for a fraction of the price, and several of them offline.

KeyStow: $5 once, offline, no account

KeyStow is the closest thing to the old Dragon promise at a hobbyist price. Hold a key (Right Ctrl on Windows, Right Option on a Mac by default; changeable), talk, release, and text appears in whatever app you are using. Local Whisper models do the recognition: your voice is processed privately on your own computer and never sent to the cloud, and dictation works without internet too. KeyStow does not keep an audio recording or transcript history. It costs $5 once for a lifetime license, runs on Windows and Apple Silicon Mac, starts at sign-in, and is MIT open source.

What it does not try to be: a macro system or a specialist vocabulary engine. It is dictation, done well, for the price of a coffee.

Handy: free and open source

Handy is a free, MIT-licensed push-to-talk app for Windows, macOS and Linux that runs speech models locally. It is the zero-cost way to get offline dictation, and its GitHub repository documents setup. Genuinely free with no tier gate.

Superwhisper: local processing, more modes

Superwhisper runs locally and offers a free tier, with Pro at $8.49 per month, $84.99 per year, or a $249.99 lifetime plan per its billing docs. It runs on Mac, Windows and iPhone, and its paid tiers add model choices and custom modes. The $249.99 lifetime option is the closest modern equivalent to buying Dragon in a box, at about a third of Dragon Professional's price.

Wispr Flow: the cloud option

Wispr Flow is the most polished cloud-based tool: automatic editing and formatting as you speak, apps for Mac, Windows, iPhone and Android, a free tier, and Pro at $15 per user per month billed monthly or $12 per user per month billed annually. Its privacy page states that transcription always happens in the cloud, so it is the wrong choice for anyone who came to Dragon for on-machine processing.

Built-in voice typing: already installed

Windows 11's voice typing (Win+H) and Apple's Dictation are free and need no install. Microsoft's support page notes Win+H uses an online speech service; Apple's page notes on-device dictation depends on your Mac and settings. Fine for quick messages, not a Dragon replacement for long-form work.

Which one replaces Dragon for you

  • Everyday writing, private and cheap: KeyStow ($5 once) or Handy (free).
  • Local processing with advanced modes: Superwhisper.
  • Cloud convenience and mobile apps, subscription acceptable: Wispr Flow.
  • Specialist vocabularies and enterprise support on Windows: Dragon Professional v16 ($699) still exists and still does this best.
