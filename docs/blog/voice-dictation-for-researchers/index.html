Voice Dictation for Researchers and Grad Students: Field Notes and Paper Drafts

November 24, 2026 · KeyStow · All articles

Research is a writing discipline in disguise

The experiment, the site visit, the interview: none of it exists for science until it is written down. Field notes, observation logs, literature summaries and eventually the paper itself. Most researchers have a backlog of unwritten notes at any given moment, and the backlog decays: details that were obvious in the field are illegible a month later.

Dictation closes the gap between observing and recording. Speak the field note where you stand, and the detail survives.

What researchers dictate

  • Field and lab notes. Conditions, deviations, observations, while you are still looking at them.
  • Paper summaries. Two minutes per paper after reading: the claim, the method, the one thing worth citing. Your future self writing the lit review will thank you.
  • Draft sections. Methods and results sections follow a spoken logic: what we did, what we saw, what it means. Say it, then formalize.
  • Supervisor updates. The weekly progress email, done in the walk back from the lab.

Work with the review process, not against it

A dictated draft is a draft. Read it for terminology, citations and numbers before it goes anywhere near a co-author. And check your institution's rules: local processing keeps your voice on your own computer, but your university's or lab's data policies decide which tools may touch unpublished results.
