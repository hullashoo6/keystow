How to Speak Punctuation and Formatting While Dictating

November 24, 2026 · KeyStow · All articles

The two schools of punctuation

Every dictation user picks a side. One group dictates clean prose and adds punctuation later, in the edit. The other speaks the punctuation as they go: "The meeting moved to Friday comma so bring your notes period." Neither is wrong. The second is faster once learned; the first is easier on day one.

Most people land in the middle: they speak the punctuation that changes meaning and fix the rest by hand.

The commands worth learning first

  • Period and comma. The two that carry a sentence. Learn these and nothing else on day one.
  • Question mark and exclamation point. Tone lives here; do not leave them to the edit.
  • New paragraph. The single highest-value spoken command. Paragraph breaks are structure, and structure is painful to add later.
  • Colon and open quote. For people who write dialogue, documentation or emails with lists.

Why it feels weird, and when it stops

Speaking punctuation violates a lifetime habit of treating writing as silent. Expect the first day to be halting, the third day to be merely odd, and the second week to be automatic. This is a motor skill, like touch-typing: it arrives suddenly and then never leaves.

A tip that shortens the learning curve: speak a little slower at punctuation points, not at the words themselves. The pause is for you, not the software.

What modern dictation adds

Current speech models are good at inferring punctuation from phrasing, so "speak everything" is less necessary than it was with older software. KeyStow transcribes your speech with punctuation inferred where it is obvious. It does not act on spoken commands: say “comma” and you may get the word, so add the rest in your edit. Either way, a quick read before sending stays part of the workflow.
