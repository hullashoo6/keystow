Voice Dictation in 2027: What's Local, What's Cloud, and What to Watch

November 24, 2026 · KeyStow · All articles

Where things stand, as of late 2026

This piece is a snapshot, written in late 2026, and the ground moves. But the shape of the landscape is stable enough to be useful: dictation has split into two camps, and both are getting better.

The local camp

Open speech models, led by the Whisper family, made accurate offline dictation practical on ordinary laptops. Local tools record nothing to the cloud, work without internet, and tend to charge once or not at all. The tradeoffs are real: you supply the compute, features differ between apps, and quality depends on the model size your machine comfortably runs.

The cloud camp

Cloud services send your audio to their servers and return text, usually for a subscription. In exchange they carry no local install burden, update their models centrally, and often bundle extras like AI rewriting. The tradeoffs: your voice leaves your machine, a connection is required, and the cost never ends.

What is worth watching in 2027

  • Local models keep shrinking. Each generation squeezes more accuracy into less compute, which steadily erases the quality argument for the cloud on everyday hardware.
  • Privacy rules keep tightening. As more workplaces formalize where voice data may go, "processed on your own device" becomes a simpler answer than a compliance review.
  • Hybrids multiply. Tools that transcribe locally but call the cloud for extras are growing. The useful question stays the same: which parts of my audio or text leave this machine, and when?
  • Pricing pressure. One-time-purchase local tools exist next to subscriptions, and buyers increasingly notice the difference over a year or two.
