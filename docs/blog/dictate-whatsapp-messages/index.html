How to Dictate WhatsApp, Telegram and Slack Messages on Your Computer

November 24, 2026 · KeyStow · All articles

Chats are the new inbox

A surprising share of daily writing now happens in chat boxes: WhatsApp Web, Telegram Desktop, Slack, Discord, Teams. Most of it is short, but it is constant, and it fragments the day into hundreds of tiny typing sessions. Dictation fits chat perfectly because chat messages are spoken language anyway. You are literally typing things you would say.

The flow, once, everywhere

With a system-wide dictation tool there is nothing to set up per app:

1. Open WhatsApp Web, Telegram Desktop, Slack or any other chat.

2. Click the message box as usual.

3. Hold Right Option (KeyStow's default shortcut, changeable), speak the message, release. The text appears in the box.

4. Glance, send.

The same habit works in every app, because KeyStow types into whatever is focused. Your voice is processed privately on your own computer and never sent to the cloud, dictation works without internet too, and KeyStow does not keep an audio recording or transcript history. It costs $5 once, runs on Windows and Apple Silicon Mac, and is MIT open source.

Why private processing matters more in chat

Chats are personal. Family logistics, work decisions, health topics, money. A dictation tool that sends audio to the cloud (Wispr Flow's privacy page says its transcription always happens there) means every dictated message takes a detour through a third party. Local tools like KeyStow, Handy and Superwhisper keep the audio on your machine, which is the simplest way to keep chat chat.

Tips for dictated chat

  • One message per hold. Chat is short-form; a sentence or two per press is the natural unit.
  • Say it like you would say it. Chat is casual, and dictated text comes out casual. That is the right register.
  • Keyboard for the fiddly bits. Emojis, @mentions and phone numbers are faster by hand.
