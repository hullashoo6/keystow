Mac's Built-in Dictation vs an Offline Dictation App

November 24, 2026 · KeyStow · All articles

What your Mac already does

macOS includes a Dictation feature that types what you say into most text fields. On recent Macs much of it runs on-device, and Apple documents which parts do. It is free, already installed, and genuinely useful for occasional short texts.

If it already works for you, keep using it. The honest question is what a dedicated app adds.

Where the built-in option runs out

  • The press-and-talk flow. Built-in dictation toggles on and off with its own trigger and timeout behavior. A hold-to-talk key gives you deliberate control: the microphone listens exactly while you hold a key and never otherwise.
  • Model choice and accuracy control. A dedicated app lets you pick between speech model sizes to balance speed and accuracy. KeyStow ships with the base model inside the installer and offers six Whisper sizes in settings.
  • A record of your throughput. KeyStow's dashboard shows how many words you have dictated, which turns "is this worth it" into a measurable answer.
  • Transparency. KeyStow is open source under the MIT license, so its privacy behavior is inspectable, not just asserted.

Where the built-in option wins

It is already there, it costs nothing, it supports many languages (KeyStow transcribes English), and it is maintained by Apple. For a dictated text message twice a week, installing anything would be overkill.

The honest verdict

Occasional short dictation: use what is on the Mac. Daily writing by voice, a deliberate hold-to-talk habit, English only: that is what KeyStow is for. It costs $5 once, with no subscription and no account.
