Voice Dictation Terms, Explained: Whisper, Offline, Hold-to-Talk and More

November 24, 2026 · KeyStow · All articles

Dictation app marketing throws around a lot of vocabulary. Here is what the common terms actually mean, in plain language.

The speech engine terms

  • Speech recognition (ASR): the technology that turns audio into text. ASR stands for automatic speech recognition.
  • Whisper: an open-source speech recognition model family released by OpenAI under the MIT license. It runs on ordinary computers, which is why so many modern offline dictation apps, KeyStow and Handy among them, use it. Its models come in sizes (tiny, base, small, large); bigger models are more accurate and need more compute.
  • faster-whisper: a popular optimized version of Whisper that runs efficiently on everyday hardware. KeyStow bundles a faster-whisper base model and lets you switch sizes in its settings.
  • On-device / offline / local processing: the audio is transcribed by a model on your computer, not sent to a server. If the app still works with wifi off, it is genuinely local.
  • Cloud transcription: your audio travels to a company's servers and text comes back. Wispr Flow's privacy page, for example, says transcription always happens in the cloud.

The interaction terms

  • Hold-to-talk (push-to-talk): the microphone listens only while you hold a key. KeyStow works this way (Right Ctrl on Windows, Right Option on a Mac by default; changeable).
  • Always-listening: the app transcribes continuously once enabled. Hands-free, but the mic is on.
  • Wake word: a phrase that activates listening ("Hey ..."). Common in smart speakers, rare in dictation apps.
  • Punctuation commands: saying "comma" or "new paragraph" to insert formatting as you speak.

The measurement terms

  • Word error rate (WER): the standard accuracy metric for speech recognition: how many words it gets wrong per hundred. Lower is better.
  • Latency: the delay between speaking and text appearing. Local models and cloud services both have some; you notice it when it breaks your flow.

Why the local/cloud split matters most

Every other term is a feature choice. Local versus cloud is a privacy architecture. A local app like KeyStow ($5 once, lifetime, Windows and Mac) processes your voice on your own computer, never sends it to the cloud, works without internet, and keeps no audio recording or transcript history. Cloud tools may be excellent, but the trade is structural, not a setting.
