Dictating Numbers, Email Addresses and URLs Without the Mess

November 24, 2026 · KeyStow · All articles

The tricky ten percent

Dictation handles prose beautifully and stumbles predictably on the rest: phone numbers that become words, emails that gain spaces, "fifty" heard as "fifteen." None of this is a reason to stop dictating. It is a reason to learn four habits and one rule.

Four speaking habits for tricky tokens

  • Spell addresses character by character when it matters. "j dot smith at company dot com" arrives clean; rattling it off at email speed does not.
  • Say numbers in digits, not words, for anything exact. "Five point two percent" is safer than trusting inference, and long numbers deserve digit-by-digit treatment: "one eight hundred, five five five, zero one two three."
  • Disambiguate homophone-prone numbers. Fifteen and fifty, eight and "ate": if the number matters, glance at it before moving on.
  • Use the keyboard for the worst offenders. Complicated URLs, serial numbers and codes are faster to type than to say three times. Choosing the right tool per token is the skill.

The one rule: verify before it counts

Anything a number or address touches (an invoice, a booking, a wire, a date) gets read with your eyes before you act on it. Dictation drafts at speaking speed; accuracy at decision points is a separate, deliberate step. Keep them separate and both stay fast.
