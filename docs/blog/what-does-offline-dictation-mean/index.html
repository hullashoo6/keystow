What Offline Dictation Actually Means (and What Still Uses the Internet)

November 24, 2026 · KeyStow · All articles

The phrase gets stretched

"Works offline" can mean three very different things: the whole speech model runs on your device, a model downloads once and then runs locally, or the app merely queues your audio and sends it when a connection returns. Only the first two keep your voice off other people's servers.

What genuinely runs on your device

A real offline dictation app carries a speech recognition model on your machine. Your microphone audio goes into that model, text comes out, and nothing about the audio leaves the computer. You can pull the network cable and dictation keeps working. That is the test that matters.

What may still use the internet

  • The initial install. Downloading the app itself obviously uses the internet. Some apps also download the speech model on first run rather than bundling it; after that one-time download, dictation is local.
  • App updates. New versions arrive over the internet, though updating is a choice, not a constant stream of your data.
  • License checks and accounts. Some tools phone home to validate a subscription or login even when recognition is local. A tool with no account has nothing to check in with.
  • Extras around the edges. Cloud backup, sync or AI rewriting features are separate from recognition and may send text away. Know which features you have switched on.

How to check before you rely on it

Turn off Wi-Fi and dictate a paragraph. If it works, recognition is local. If it spins, fails or quietly degrades, something was calling home.
