What Is Whisper? The Open-Source Speech Model Behind Offline Dictation

November 24, 2026 · KeyStow · All articles

If you have looked at offline dictation apps lately, you have seen one name repeated: Whisper. KeyStow, Superwhisper, Handy and dozens of other tools all use it. Here is what Whisper actually is, and why it changed what a $5 dictation app can do.

Whisper in one paragraph

Whisper is a general-purpose speech recognition model released by OpenAI in 2022. Both the code and the model weights are public under the MIT license, which means anyone can download it, run it on their own hardware, and build products on top of it, for free. That is the whole reason offline dictation apps exist at consumer prices.

What it can do

Per OpenAI's repository, Whisper is a multitasking model: beyond transcribing speech to text in many languages, it can also translate speech into English and identify which language is being spoken. It is a Transformer sequence-to-sequence model trained on a large, diverse audio dataset, so it handles accents, background noise and technical vocabulary better than older open models.

Sizes: tiny to large, plus turbo

Whisper is not one model but a family. OpenAI publishes it in sizes from tiny to large, plus a fast "turbo" variant. The trade-off is simple: smaller models run faster and need less memory; larger models are more accurate. This is why apps like KeyStow let you pick a model size in settings: on a fast machine you can run a bigger model for better accuracy, on an older laptop a small one is the practical choice.

Why it matters for privacy

Before Whisper, good dictation mostly meant sending your microphone audio to a company's servers, because the accurate models only ran in data centers. Whisper made high-quality transcription something your own laptop can do. An app that runs Whisper locally can transcribe your voice with no internet connection and no audio ever leaving your computer. That is exactly how KeyStow works: Whisper runs on your machine, your voice is processed privately on your own computer and never sent to the cloud.
