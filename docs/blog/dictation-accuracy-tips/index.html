How to Speak for Speech Recognition: Mic, Pace and Environment

November 24, 2026 · KeyStow · All articles

The tool is only half the equation

Two people use the same dictation app and report opposite results. The difference is rarely the software; it is how they speak to it. Modern speech models like Whisper are remarkably capable, but they work with the audio you give them. A few habits change that audio a lot.

Microphone basics

  • Use the best mic you have within reason. A headset or decent USB mic beats a laptop's built-in mic across the room, but a laptop mic at normal desk distance is fine for most models.
  • Keep the distance consistent. Speech models dislike volume swings more than they dislike modest volume. Pick a distance and keep it.
  • Aim past, not into. Position the mic slightly to the side of your mouth so breath plosives ("p" and "b" pops) miss it.

How to speak

  • Talk like a podcast host, not a auctioneer. A natural, slightly deliberate pace beats both mumbling and machine-gun delivery.
  • Finish your sentences in your head first. Half-restarted sentences ("so we should, actually, the plan is") transcribe exactly as badly as they sound. One breath of thought before holding the key saves editing later.
  • Say punctuation if your tool uses it. "Comma" and "period" feel silly for a day, then disappear into habit.
  • Do not over-enunciate. Deliberately robot-speech often confuses models trained on natural speech. Speak like a person.

Environment

  • Quiet beats everything. A fan, traffic or a TV in the background is the single biggest accuracy killer. Close the window before you buy a better mic.
  • Soft rooms sound better. Bare walls and hard desks create echo; a room with a rug, curtains or a bookshelf gives the model cleaner audio.

The feedback loop

After each session, skim what the model got wrong. Everyone has personal trouble words: names, jargon, mumbled endings. Keep a short list of yours and either pronounce those words a touch more clearly or plan to fix them in the edit. Accuracy improves fastest when you treat the first week as training for you, not for the software.
