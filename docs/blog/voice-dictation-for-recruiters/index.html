Voice Dictation for Recruiters: Candidate Notes and Outreach at Speed

November 24, 2026 · KeyStow · All articles

The job is conversations, the record is text

A recruiter's day is phone screens and interviews, but the hiring manager sees notes and the candidate sees messages. The gap between "great call, must remember why" and a written summary is where good candidates get misremembered and bland outreach gets sent.

The fix is to close the gap immediately. Sixty seconds of dictation after each call captures what a typed note at 6 PM never will.

What recruiters dictate

  • Post-call candidate notes. Strengths, concerns, salary expectations, notice period, the one detail that will sell this person to the hiring manager. Speak it the moment you hang up.
  • Interview debriefs. Structured feedback for the hiring panel while the conversation is fresh, pasted into your ATS notes field.
  • Outreach that sounds human. Dictated messages read like speech, which is exactly why they get replies. Say why this candidate, why this role, then tidy the names and links.
  • Hiring-manager updates. The weekly pipeline summary: who moved, who stalled, who needs a decision.

A note on candidate data

Candidate conversations include personal details people did not publish. A cloud dictation tool sends that audio to a third party. KeyStow processes voice on your own computer and keeps no recordings or transcript history. That is a real privacy difference, but your firm's data-handling policy and any agreements with clients still govern which tools you may use. Check them first.

How KeyStow fits

KeyStow is an offline voice dictation app for Windows and Apple Silicon Mac. Hold Right Ctrl on Windows (Right Option on a Mac), speak, release, and the text is pasted where your cursor is, including your ATS, email and LinkedIn message boxes. Your voice is processed privately on your own computer and never sent to the cloud, and dictation works without internet too.
