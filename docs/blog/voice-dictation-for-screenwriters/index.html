Voice Dictation for Screenwriters: Dialogue That Sounds Spoken

November 24, 2026 · KeyStow · All articles

Dialogue is a spoken art

Typed dialogue tends to come out written: complete sentences, tidy grammar, nobody interrupting anyone. Spoken dialogue comes out alive, because that is what it is. Screenwriters have read lines aloud for a century to catch this; dictation simply records the read-aloud.

The workflow

  • Speak the scene, characters included. Do both sides of the exchange yourself. Acting the argument produces different words than composing it, and the difference is usually better.
  • Mark the speaker as you go. Say the character name before the line, the way a table read does. Sorting speakers afterward is easy when the name is in the audio's transcript.
  • Format after, not during. Screenplay formatting demands precision dictation is bad at. Draft the scene as raw spoken text, then shape it in your screenwriting software where the formatting rules live.
  • Keep the golden accidents. The stumbled phrase, the half-finished thought, the interruption you did not plan: read them before deleting. Some of the best lines arrive as mistakes.

Where dictation earns its place

First drafts of dialogue-heavy scenes, brainstorming loglines and beat notes aloud, and capturing a revision idea the moment it arrives. It earns less in the formatting pass and the final polish, where precision beats speed.
