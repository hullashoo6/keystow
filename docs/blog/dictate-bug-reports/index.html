How to Dictate Bug Reports That Get Fixed Faster

November 24, 2026 · KeyStow · All articles

Bugs are fixed by details, and details evaporate

The difference between "cannot reproduce" and "fixed in an hour" is usually the quality of the report: what you clicked, what you expected, what happened instead, what the screen showed. Those details are sharpest in the sixty seconds after the bug appears, which is exactly when nobody wants to stop and type.

Dictation resolves the tension. Without leaving the screen, you can narrate the whole report: hold a key, describe what just happened, release.

The anatomy of a dictated bug report

  • What you were doing. "Checkout page, applied a discount code, hit pay." Context first.
  • What you expected. One sentence. This is what makes the bug a bug.
  • What happened instead. The error text, the frozen spinner, the wrong total. Read error messages aloud exactly.
  • The environment. Browser, OS, account type, anything unusual about your setup.
  • Repro steps, numbered out loud. "One: log in as a free user. Two: add two items..." If you can say the steps, someone else can follow them.

For QA, support and everyone between

Support agents reporting upstream, QA testers logging a session's worth of findings, developers documenting their own regressions: the pattern holds. Dictate at the scene, paste into the tracker, attach the screenshot. Then skim once: product names and version numbers must be exact.
