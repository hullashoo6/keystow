The Five-Minute Status Update: Dictate Your Weekly Report

November 24, 2026 · KeyStow · All articles

Friday at 5 PM deserves better

The weekly status update is the most recycled chore in office life: due every Friday, written in a hurry, read by exactly one busy person. So it gets the worst version of your attention, or none. Either way, the work you actually did goes undersold.

The trick is to stop writing it and start saying it. You could explain your week to a colleague in two minutes without notes. That explanation is the update.

The four-part template

Hold the key and answer four questions out loud, one short paragraph each:

  • Shipped. What actually got done, named concretely. Not "worked on the migration" but "moved the staging database, hit one snag with indices."
  • In progress. What is halfway and when it lands.
  • Blocked. What needs someone else's decision, named directly. This paragraph is why your manager reads the update.
  • Next. The one to three things that matter next week.

Then spend your saved minutes on the only step that cannot be spoken: checking names, dates and numbers.

Why this works better than a template you paste

Pasted templates get pasted filler. A dictated answer to "what did I ship this week" forces specifics because you are answering a question, not filling a box. The update gets shorter, more concrete and more honest, and it takes five minutes instead of thirty.
