Voice Typing Without the Cloud: Why Local Dictation Matters for Privacy

November 24, 2026 · KeyStow · All articles

When you dictate, your microphone picks up more than words. Names, addresses, client details, medical notes, passwords read aloud, the TV in the background. Where does all of that go? The answer depends on one design choice: whether the app transcribes in the cloud or on your own computer.

What happens to your voice with cloud dictation

Most popular dictation tools send your audio to a server. The server's speech model transcribes it and sends text back. Two examples, from the companies' own pages:

  • Wispr Flow's privacy page says "Transcription always happens in the cloud to provide the best speed and accuracy." The same page explains that, depending on your settings, your dictation data may be used to evaluate, train or improve AI models, and that optional cloud storage keeps transcripts and audio on Wispr's servers for cross-device features.
  • Windows voice typing (Windows key + H) "uses online speech recognition, which is powered by Azure Speech services," per Microsoft's support page, and it needs an internet connection to work at all.

Cloud transcription is not automatically bad. Big providers encrypt data in transit and at rest, and some let you opt out of training use. But it does mean your voice leaves your computer every time you speak, you rely on a company's retention settings being configured the way you think they are, and dictation stops working the moment your connection does.

What local dictation does instead

Local, or offline, dictation runs the speech recognition model on your own device. Modern open models like OpenAI's Whisper are good enough to run on an ordinary laptop, so there is no longer a quality reason to ship audio to a server for everyday dictation.

With local processing:

  • Your audio never leaves your computer, so there is nothing in transit to intercept and nothing on a server to breach.
  • Dictation works without internet: on a plane, in a dead zone, on a metered connection.
  • Retention is simple. KeyStow, for example, does not keep an audio recording or transcript history at all. There is nothing to delete later because nothing was kept.

Who benefits most

  • Anyone dictating sensitive text: legal, medical, HR or client material that should not visit a third-party server.
  • People who work offline: travel, field work, unreliable connections.
  • Anyone who prefers simple data hygiene: the easiest recording to protect is the one that was never uploaded.
