When Typing Beats Talking: Dictating Discreetly in Shared Spaces

November 24, 2026 · KeyStow · All articles

The honest case for the keyboard

Dictation advice usually skips the obvious objection: most people do not work alone in a soundproof room. Open offices, coworking spaces, libraries, a kitchen table with someone on a call two meters away. In those places, talking to your laptop is somewhere between rude and impossible, and no productivity tip changes that.

So the first rule of sustainable dictation is knowing when not to. Typing is not a failure mode; it is the right tool for shared, silent and sensitive spaces.

Making dictation work around other people

  • Find the gaps, not the silence. Early morning, the empty meeting room, the car at lunch, the walk to the station. Ten dictated minutes in a gap beat an hour of frustrated typing at a shared desk.
  • Drop your volume before your accuracy. Speech models handle a low conversational voice well. You do not need to project; a murmur at arm's length from the mic is usually plenty.
  • Split the workflow. Dictate drafts in private moments, edit silently in public ones. The keyboard work (editing, links, formatting) is exactly the work that suits a quiet open office.
  • Save the keyboard for the sensitive stuff. Personnel matters, passwords-adjacent details, anything you would not say aloud anyway.

Shared homes count too

Working from home with family around is its own etiquette. Dictating during someone's movie is a household decision, not a productivity one. The car, the early hour and the empty room apply here as much as in any office.

How KeyStow fits

KeyStow is an offline voice dictation app for Windows and Apple Silicon Mac. Hold Right Ctrl on Windows (Right Option on a Mac), speak, release, and the text is pasted where your cursor is. Because your voice is processed privately on your own computer and never sent to the cloud, dictation works without internet too, including in a parked car with no signal.
