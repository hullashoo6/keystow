How to Take Conference and Webinar Notes by Voice

November 24, 2026 · KeyStow · All articles

Day-two amnesia is real

Every conference follows the same arc: day one you take notes, day two you take photos of slides you will never look at, day three you fly home with a tote bag and a vague sense that something was useful. The value of the event evaporates because nobody has the energy to type notes between sessions.

You do have thirty seconds though. Enough to step into the hallway and dictate: what the talk was about, the one idea worth keeping, the person to follow up with.

A conference capture system

  • One note per session. Title, speaker, the single best idea, one action. Thirty to sixty seconds, dictated between sessions.
  • Capture people, not just content. "Met Priya from Acme, hiring for their docs team, send her the integration write-up." Said out loud immediately, it becomes a task; remembered on the plane, it becomes nothing.
  • Dictate the recap on the way home. Ten minutes in the taxi: top three takeaways, who to email, what to try first. This note is the return on the ticket price.
  • Webinars count. Mute your mic, keep the cursor in your notes, dictate your own takeaways during the Q&A when the content is settled.

Manners and privacy

Dictate in hallways and quiet corners, not in the audience. Your notes are your own thoughts about the session; that is different from recording the speaker, which many events forbid.
