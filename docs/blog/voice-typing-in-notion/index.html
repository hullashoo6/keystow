Voice Typing in Notion: Your Real Options

November 24, 2026 · KeyStow · All articles

Notion's dictation gap

Notion is where a lot of writing lives now: docs, wikis, meeting notes, project plans. But on desktop, Notion has no full built-in dictation feature. On mobile you can use the phone keyboard's voice typing, which sends audio to Apple or Google. On the desktop app or browser, the official answer is your operating system's tools.

That leaves a real gap for anyone who thinks out loud: Notion holds the document, but getting speech into it takes another tool.

The system-wide approach

Because Notion is a text field like any other, a system-wide dictation app fills the gap cleanly. The cursor goes in the Notion page, you talk, the text lands in the block. No plugin, no export step, no integration to maintain.

This approach also covers the places plugins forget: database cells, comments, the page title itself.

Why offline matters here

Notion pages tend to hold the sensitive stuff: plans, decisions, people notes. A dictation tool that sends audio to the cloud means every dictated page takes a detour through a third party. KeyStow runs the speech model on your computer: your voice is processed privately on your own computer and never sent to the cloud, and dictation works without internet too. KeyStow does not keep an audio recording or transcript history.

How KeyStow fits

KeyStow is an offline voice dictation app for Windows and Apple Silicon Mac. Hold Right Ctrl on Windows (Right Option on a Mac), speak, release, and the text is typed into whatever block your cursor is in. The speech model ships inside the installer, so there is no model download on first use. It costs $5 once, with no subscription and no account, and the source code is open under the MIT license.
