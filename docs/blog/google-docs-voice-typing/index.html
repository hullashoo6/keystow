Google Docs Voice Typing vs an Offline Dictation App

November 24, 2026 · KeyStow · All articles

What Google Docs gives you

Open a Google Doc in Chrome, click Tools > Voice typing, and speak. It is free, built in, and good enough that many people never look further. Its limits are just as built in:

1. It only works in Google Docs, in Chrome. Not in your email, not in Slack, not in a Word file, not in a browser form.

2. It needs the internet. Google Docs itself is a cloud app; voice typing rides the same connection.

3. Your audio goes to Google's servers. That is how the feature works, covered by Google's own policies.

For quick notes inside Docs at a connected desk, it is hard to beat free and zero setup.

Where it stops helping

  • The moment you leave Docs: your email reply, your chat message, your CMS editor, your project tracker all need typing again.
  • The moment you lose connection: a flight, a cabin, a conference basement.
  • The moment the content is sensitive: voice typing in Docs means your spoken words take the same cloud trip as the document.

The system-wide offline option: KeyStow

KeyStow ($5 once, lifetime) is a hold-to-talk dictation app for Windows and Mac that runs Whisper speech models on your device. It types into whatever is focused: Google Docs included, plus every other app and text field on your computer.

  • Offline: no connection needed, ever.
  • Private: your voice is processed privately on your own computer and never sent to the cloud. Dictation works without internet too. KeyStow does not keep an audio recording or transcript history.
  • One habit: the same shortcut key in Docs, Gmail, Slack, Word and everything else, instead of a different feature per app.
  • Free local alternatives: Handy (free, open source) and Superwhisper (free local tier, Pro from $8.49 per month) also keep audio on your machine.

Which one for which writer

  • You live entirely inside Google Docs, always online, and your content is fine in the cloud: built-in voice typing is enough.
  • You write across apps, travel, or handle sensitive material: a system-wide offline tool earns its $5 fast.
  • You want both: many people keep Docs voice typing for Docs at the desk and KeyStow as the everywhere-else default.
