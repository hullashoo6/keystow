Buying Dictation Software for a Small Team: A Checklist

November 24, 2026 · KeyStow · All articles

Team buying is a different job

Choosing a dictation app for yourself takes an afternoon. Buying for a team means other people's voices, other people's data and usually other people's rules. The checklist below is the short version of what to settle before anyone enters a card number.

The checklist

  • Where does the audio go? Cloud tools send voice to outside servers. Offline tools process it on the machine. For client work, NDAs or regulated industries, this single question often decides the shortlist. Your own policies make the call, not the vendor's marketing page.
  • What is the real cost model? Per-seat subscriptions look small monthly and large annually. Multiply by headcount and by years before comparing against one-time purchases.
  • Who can install it? Locked-down laptops need IT approval for anything new. Check whether the tool needs admin rights before promising it to the team.
  • Can your security team inspect it? Open-source code can be audited; closed code cannot. Some teams require this, and finding out late wastes everyone's time.
  • What happens to transcripts? Ask whether the tool keeps recordings or history, and whether that fits your retention rules.
  • Is there an exit? If the team stops paying, do people lose the tool, their custom settings or their dictionary?

Run a pilot before the rollout

Two or three volunteers for two weeks will answer questions no checklist can: whether people actually use it, and whether shared-office norms need adjusting first.
