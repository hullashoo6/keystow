Voice Dictation for Podcasters and YouTubers: Scripts, Show Notes and Descriptions

November 24, 2026 · KeyStow · All articles

Creators already speak for a living

Podcasters and YouTubers have an odd writing problem: their medium is speech, but everything around the medium is text. Scripts, outlines, show notes, episode descriptions, titles, newsletters, sponsor reads, social posts. Talking is the easy part; the typing around it is the tax.

Dictation flips that. You can draft a script by performing it, which is also the best possible test of whether it sounds natural out loud, because it was out loud.

Where dictation fits a creator workflow

  • Scripts and talking points: speak the episode as if recording a rough take. You will hear immediately which lines are unpronounceable, which is exactly what a script needs to catch.
  • Show notes: after recording, dictate the summary and timestamps while the episode is fresh instead of re-listening at double speed.
  • Descriptions and titles: say five title options out loud and keep the one that sounds like something you would click.
  • Sponsor reads and CTAs: dictated ad reads keep a conversational tone that typed ones lose.

Unreleased content is a privacy case

Pre-publication episodes, sponsor details and embargoed guest announcements are exactly what you do not want processed by a third party before release. KeyStow ($5 once, lifetime, Windows and Apple Silicon Mac) runs Whisper speech models offline on your computer: your voice is processed privately on your own machine and never sent to the cloud, it works without internet, and no audio recording or transcript history is kept. Handy (free, open source) is another local option. Note that local processing settles where your dictation audio goes; sponsor contracts and embargoes still govern what you can share and with whom.

One honest limit: dictation drafts your text. It is not a transcription tool for your recorded episodes; that is a different job with different tools.

A fast starting habit

Next episode, dictate the outline instead of typing it. If the outline sounds good spoken, the episode usually follows.
