Offline Dictation Without a GPU: How CPU Transcription Works

November 24, 2026 · KeyStow · All articles

The GPU myth

Because AI headlines revolve around giant data-center GPUs, many people assume local speech recognition needs a gaming rig. It does not. Dictation transcribes one voice in real time, which is a far smaller job than training a model, and modern laptop processors handle it comfortably.

What the CPU actually does

When you speak, the app slices your audio into short windows and runs them through the speech model on the processor. The model sizes used for everyday dictation are small enough that an ordinary laptop CPU transcribes faster than you talk. You speak for ten seconds, and the text is ready in a moment after you stop.

What changes with bigger models

Larger speech models are more accurate with accents, noise and rare words, and they need more compute. On a CPU they still work; they just take longer per chunk. A discrete GPU speeds up the big models, which matters for batch-transcribing hours of recordings. For live dictation with a small or standard model, it is rarely the bottleneck.

Practical guidance

  • Start with the default or smaller model. If it keeps up and the accuracy suits you, stop there.
  • Step up only if you hear the errors. Heavy accents or noisy rooms may justify a larger model; quiet offices usually do not.
  • Do not buy hardware for dictation. Any laptop that runs a modern browser comfortably will run offline dictation.
